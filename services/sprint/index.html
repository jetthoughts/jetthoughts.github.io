A written scope

A feature that isn't written down grows while it's being built, and so does the bill.

  • One page, one price. The engineer who scopes it builds it, and the price is fixed in writing on a one-page scope before work starts.
  • Free to scope. Scoping is free: one 30-minute call and a read-only look at your repo, with a written scope within 2 working days.
  • A short look first. Read-only: the parts of the code the feature touches, the tests and the deploy path. Half a day at most.
  • Two fixed weeks. The scope is agreed in writing before we start. Anything new becomes a second Sprint at the same $3,200.
  • Not for. App-store releases, third-party approvals, migrations with no rollback, and stacks we haven't shipped to production.

Live on production

A feature that works on a laptop and stalls before release isn't done.

  • Done means production. Done means the acceptance criterion passes on production, behind a flag you can turn on if you prefer.
  • Mid-sprint. A staging link and a mid-sprint call.
  • If it slips. Half is paid at signing, half when it passes on production. If the agreed scope is not live in ten working days, we work up to 5 more days at no charge.
  • A dated start. Work starts on a dated Monday, usually within two weeks of signing.
