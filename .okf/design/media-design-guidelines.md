# Course Media Design Guidelines

**Source:** External media-design research doc (2026-07-26). Applied to the
Tech for Non-Technical Founders course via
`docs/projects/2605-tech-for-non-technical-founders/10-19-research/10.06-media-design-recommendations.md`.

---

## 1. Objectives and role of media

Media in a business course should do three things: **clarify concepts, drive
engagement, and support application to real business scenarios**. Every asset
(slide, video, chart, worksheet) must be traceable to a specific learning
objective — not just decoration.

### Requirements

- Map each media item to a clear outcome (e.g. "Explain unit economics",
  "Practice stakeholder mapping").
- Prefer examples from real or realistic businesses over abstract cases.
- Design media so learners can reuse it at work (templates, canvases,
  checklists).

---

## 2. Media types to include

Bundle for each module: **Explain → Show → Practice → Reflect**.

### Recommended media mix

| Type | Purpose | Format constraints |
|------|---------|-------------------|
| **Slide decks** | Minimal text, 1 key idea per slide, diagrams instead of paragraphs | 12–15 slides per micro-module |
| **Short explainer videos** | 3–7 min, one concept each, simple animations | 1 core concept per video; hook → concept → example → CTA |
| **Data visuals** | Charts/tables for KPIs, unit economics, funnels, org charts | Clear legends, plain-language titles |
| **Templates & canvases** | Fillable PDFs or online forms (BMC, stakeholder map, etc.) | Single-page preferred; example + blank versions |
| **Scenario sheets** | 1–2 page cases for discussion or exercises | Concrete numbers, realistic scenarios |
| **Checklists** | Before/after meeting, decision, project checklists | Scaffold behavior change, not info overload |

## 3. Design guidelines (visual and UX)

Aim for **"corporate-ready but simple"** — professional, easy to consume
during a busy workday.

### Visual standards

- **Consistent branding:** same color palette, typography, layout across
  all modules.
- **High-resolution, relevant images only;** avoid cliché stock photos and
  unnecessary decoration.
- **One primary idea per slide/screen;** keep whitespace, avoid clutter.
- **Charts must be legible:** limited series, clear legends, plain-language
  titles ("Why churn is killing profit").

### Accessibility and usability

- Clear, large fonts and high contrast colors.
- Downloadable versions (PDF or slides) for offline review.
- Caption all videos; provide transcripts for text-first learners.
- Keep file sizes reasonable; standard formats (PDF, MP4, PPTX).

## 4. Content and interaction specifications

Business learners engage most when media is tightly tied to **decisions,
trade-offs, and real constraints**.

### Content standards

- Use concrete numbers and simple scenarios (e.g. "SMB SaaS with 1000
  customers, €50 MRR each") rather than vague examples.
- Show both **"good" and "bad"** examples (effective vs ineffective
  stakeholder emails, strong vs weak KPI dashboard).
- Always highlight the **decision point:** "What would you do?", "Which
  option would you choose and why?".
- Connect each concept to a **practical tool** (spreadsheet, template,
  email script, meeting agenda).

### Interaction patterns per module

1. **1–2 short questions** learners answer using the media.
2. **1 small task:** fill a canvas, run a calculation, rewrite an email.
3. **1 reflection:** "Where in your current job could you apply this this
   week?"

---

## 5. Recommended specifications by format

### Slides

- Max 12–15 slides per micro-module (≈10–15 min teaching).
- 1 headline, 1 visual, minimal supporting text per slide.
- Consistent layout patterns: **Problem → Visual → Insight → Action**.
- Embed or link to templates/worksheets from the slide where introduced.

### Videos

- 3–7 min each; 1 core concept per video.
- Structure: **hook (real problem) → concept explanation → worked example
  → CTA** ("Do this in your worksheet now").
- Simple motion graphics; highlighted numbers/flows.
- Always pair a video with a concrete exercise or worksheet.

### Charts and data media

- Show only necessary metrics for the story (revenue, cost, churn, CAC,
  LTV).
- Include a plain-language **"caption"** explaining what to look at.
- Avoid 3D charts or overly complex visualizations.

### Templates and canvases

- Single-page whenever possible; clear sections and prompts.
- Provide an **example-filled version** plus a **blank version**.
- Use language that matches the rest of the course (no jargon drift).
- Design for print or digital use (fillable fields or simple tables).

---

## 6. Recommendations for sequencing and engagement

Standard pattern for each module:

1. **Start** with a short scenario or problem ("You are managing X; Y
   has happened").
2. **Introduce** the concept that solves the problem (slide or video).
3. **Show** a chart or diagram that makes the concept tangible.
4. **Have learners apply** it using a template/canvas (to the scenario or
   their own workplace).
5. **Close** with reflection/discussion prompt + checklist for real-life
   application.
