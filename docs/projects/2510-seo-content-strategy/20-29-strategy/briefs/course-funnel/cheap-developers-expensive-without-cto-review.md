## Angle (updated 2026-08-19 - AI supersedes the cheap-human framing)

The shipped post's premise was a cheap *human* developer nobody reviews. The live
version of that problem is AI, and it is categorically worse in one specific way:

> With a cheap developer you at least had a person who made the choices and could be
> asked why. AI removes the author. There is nobody to interrogate, and it answers
> with identical confidence whether it was right or not.

**This is the non-duplicating hook** - it is what none of the six existing cluster
posts say. They cover *what AI code does wrong* (debt, disposability, cost,
ownership). None covers *the missing reader*.

**The shipped post's own tactic breaks under AI, which is the proof.** Its
20-minute check is "open the pull request list and see who approved the last ten
changes." When a founder prompts AI directly there are often no pull requests at
all, or the founder approved every one. The check returns a clean-looking answer
for a codebase nobody senior has ever read.

**Replacement tactic (works under AI, gives the reader something today):** pick one
screen that touches money or user data. Ask whoever is building to explain in plain
words what happens when two people use it at the same time, and what happens when
someone sends bad input. Not what the code says - what happens. "The AI handled
that", or going away and returning with more AI output, is the answer that tells you
nobody senior is in the loop.

Bridge to the course as pre-hire prevention: run the decision tree first, screen with
the interview script if you do hire.
