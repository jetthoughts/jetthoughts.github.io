## The brief (paste from here down)

Design a homepage (then: single-service, contact, about) for a software
consultancy that rescues failed software projects. Goal: the visitor's first
impression is **"this is the top firm, with a top-tier site."**

**The visitor — design for exactly this person:**
Alex, 30-45, founder & CEO of a funded startup ($100K-$2M raised, 1-20
staff). Business background, NOT computer science — cannot evaluate code
quality or architecture. Sole decision-maker under $50K; budget $5K-$30K/mo.
Already burned: their dev shop delivered late, over budget, and buggy, and
they could not tell it was going wrong until it was. Embarrassed about that.
Suspicious of anyone technical who sounds confident. What they want: "a
transparent partner who explains everything in plain English and holds
themselves accountable — so I can stop worrying about development and focus
on growing my business."

Most arrive from a blog article, not an ad. The page answers **"who are these
people and can I trust them with my company"**, not "what is this product".

**FULLY OPEN:** propose any palette, any typography, any visual identity. You
are not bound to any existing brand colour or face. Choose what best creates
the top-tier impression for THIS visitor.

**Hard constraints (the only ones):**
- No stock photography of people. Real artifacts only — audits, reports,
  dashboards, timelines, code reviews.
- No logo walls, no testimonial carousels, no repeated "Book a call" buttons.
- Every claim showable, not asserted. (Company facts, if used, must come from
  the claims canon — founded 2008, "4.8/5 on Clutch" with profile linked, no
  review count.)
- Design from first principles for this buyer; do NOT extract from the
  current jetthoughts.com.

**The question the first screen must answer:** what does a burned,
embarrassed, suspicious founder need to SEE (not read) to think "these people
are serious, senior, and safe"? Build the page around that. One clear next
action, not six.

  manufactured dissent is not; each names the strongest finding AGAINST its

  screens, 3-line verdict per lens, one "what this says about us" sentence.

## The vote — 2026-08-21 (four personas on sonnet, fable critic in main loop)

| Voter | Vote | Decisive reason | Objection to own pick |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alex, burned founder | **D2** | "Vague empathy is what the last shop gave me" — D2's specific failure-naming (Opaque Box, Misaligned Clock, Debt Spiral) reads as competence | dark enterprise-navy is the aesthetic of the shops that burn founders; slick dashboards are cheap to fake |
| CFO skeptic | **D1** | D2's fabricated "90-day guarantee" is a due-diligence disqualifier — one invented promise poisons every other number on the page. "Vague survives a diligence call; fabrication survives it with a lie or a retraction." | D1's informality is itself a red flag at a five-figure monthly spend |
| Mobile commuter | **D1** | D2's failure is structural (illegible dashboard hero, fixed-1280 grid rework); D1's is cosmetic (headline reflow) | D1's flat-lay photography reads as agency gloss |
| Brand strategist | **D1** | D1 is a *reading system* that carries blog/course/case-studies; D2 is a landing-page costume that fights body text everywhere else | evidentiary calm might read slow to a founder bleeding runway |
| **Fable critic** (render inspection) | **D1** | D2 has visible execution defects: hero + Phase-01 + final-CTA text renders washed-out pale on WHITE (near-invisible), and the page alternates white/black bands — the exact background-switch disease 2.4 exists to cure. D1's defects (staged wood styling ×2, two CTA idioms) are cosmetic. | D1's tablet "weekly report" mock is its weakest artifact — replace with a real report render |

**Synthesis worth grafting regardless of winner:** D2's *problem-naming specificity* (Opaque Box / Misaligned Clock / Debt Spiral) is the single thing Alex trusted most — port that COPY register into D1's visual system, minus the fabricated guarantee. And D1's "VIEW A REAL AUDIT" link-as-primary-action is the best trust move on either page — keep it.
