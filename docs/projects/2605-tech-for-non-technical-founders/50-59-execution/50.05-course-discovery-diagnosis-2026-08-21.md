# 50.05 - Course discovery diagnosis (2026-08-21)

**Question asked:** why do 60+ course URLs sit at positions 5-13 with ~800
impressions and 2 clicks, while zero course URLs appear in GSC's top-200
page+query rows?

**Answer: the premise was wrong.** The course does not rank well and fail to
convert clicks. It does not rank at all, for anything a founder searches. The
"positions 5-13" figure is an averaging artifact of near-zero impressions.

Live pulls, `sc-domain:jetthoughts.com`, 2026-07-23 to 2026-08-20 (28d), plus
one 90d page pull. GA figures from `bin/site-report`, same day.

---

## 1. The positions are noise, not rankings

GSC averages position over impressions, so a page seen three times can report a
better position than a page seen three hundred times. Course rows are almost
all in the first category:

| Course page | Impressions (28d) | Reported position |
|---|---|---|
| `form-your-founding-hypothesis-90-minute-sprint` | **1** | 3.0 |
| `/course/tech-for-non-technical-founders-2026/` | **3** | 4.3 |
| `founder-os-pack.pdf` | **2** | 4.5 |
| `outreach-sequence-template` | **2** | 5.0 |
| `find-10-people-where-to-look` | **2** | 6.5 |

Reading "position 3" off a single impression and concluding the page ranks is
the error the inherited summary made. Nothing in that table is a ranking.

## 2. 92% of the course's named query impressions are junk

GSC will name only six queries across all course pages in 28 days, totalling 62
impressions:

| Query | Impressions | Position |
|---|---|---|
| `"stripe / collison" anti-reference` | 39 | 6.5 |
| `dashboard.stripe.com/screenshare` | 18 | 10.4 |
| `find me this website` | 1 | 4 |
| `pre launch checklist` | 2 | 61.5 |
| `sean ellis test` | 1 | 42 |
| `www.founders-os.com` | 1 | 97 |

The top two are 57 of 62 impressions and neither is a person looking for
founder education - one is a scraped-string query, the other is someone trying
to reach Stripe's own support screenshare. The four remaining queries total
**five impressions**, at positions 42 to 97.

Everything else is below GSC's query-reporting threshold, which is itself the
finding: the course's impressions are spread so thin across single-impression
long-tail queries that Google will not name them.

## 3. Where the course has a real keyword, it is on page 3

The one course page with an unambiguous high-volume target, over 90 days:

| Query | Impressions | Position |
|---|---|---|
| `mom test questions examples` | 7 | **26.6** |
| `the mom test questions` | 7 | **32.6** |

That is the honest number for a course page against a real query. Not 5-13.

## 4. The domain ranks fine - for dev topics only

The site is not weak in search. Same 28 days, sitewide:

| Query | Clicks | Impressions | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rails install dependencies` | 10 | 57 | 5.9 |
| `langchain tutorial` | 4 | 179 | 19.2 |
| `sidekiq vs solid queue` | 1 | 16 | 7.4 |
| `rails virtual attribute` | 1 | 15 | 3.5 |

Every real ranking the domain holds is a Ruby/Rails/AI-engineering query.
Google has classified jetthoughts.com as a dev-shop engineering domain, and it
is right. Course pages ask that domain to compete on founder-validation
keywords against founder-education sites, and they land on page 3+.

**So this is a topical-authority problem, not a CTR problem and not an
indexation problem.** The pages are indexed and served; they lose on relevance
authority. This is also why the falsified title/meta experiment
(`seo-review-2026-08-13` §6 #5) could never have worked - rewriting a snippet
cannot move a page from position 27, and there were no impressions to convert.

## 5. The channel that works needs none of that authority

`bin/site-report`, same 28 days:

| Channel | Sessions | Engaged | Eng% |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI Assistant | 34 | 27 | **79%** |
| Organic Social | 15 | 13 | 87% |
| Organic Search (real, per GSC) | ~5 clicks/day sitewide | - | - |
| LinkedIn campaign (`course_promo*`) | **2** | - | - |

`/course/tech-for-non-technical-founders-2026` is the **15th most-landed page on
the site** at 19 engaged / 23 sessions, without ranking for anything.

LLM retrieval matches on content, not on domain topical authority - which is
precisely the constraint blocking the course in Google. The course is already
winning in the channel where its handicap does not apply, and it out-delivers
the LinkedIn campaign it is being measured against by 17x.

---

## What this changes

1. **Stop treating course Google performance as a fixable gap.** It is not a
   defect to repair; it is a structural mismatch between the domain's topical
   authority and the course's subject. Any further SEO work aimed at course
   pages should be considered falsified in advance unless it changes topical
   authority, which a title pass cannot.
2. **Retire "positions 5-13" from every plan doc that quotes it.** It has been
   read as encouraging in at least two reads (Aug-14, Aug-20). It is an
   artifact. The real number is position 26-33 on the one page with a real
   keyword.
3. **AEO is the course's live channel, and it is unattended.** 34 sessions at
   79% engagement arrived with nobody working the channel. That asymmetry - the
   worked channel delivering 2, the unworked one delivering 34 - is the
   strongest signal in this pull.
4. **The LinkedIn arrival bet is being measured against the wrong baseline.**
   Kill-criteria in `linkedin-posts/metrics-ledger.md` should be read next to
   the AI-assistant number, not in isolation.

## What this does NOT say

- Not that LinkedIn should stop. It says one wave of it delivered 2 sessions
  and the comparison is now on the record.
- Not that the course content is weak. Read-depth and engagement are healthy
  wherever people arrive; 79% engagement from AI assistants is the course
  content doing its job.
- Not that the course should be de-indexed or moved. Nothing here argues for a
  structural change to URLs - that was ADR-0003's territory and is unaffected.
