A founder showed me an MVP he'd paid $40k for. Three months of work from cheap developers he found on Upwork.

I'd built basically the same product a couple of years ago - a marketplace for specialists - so I ran a real audit on his. It worked, sort of. Not production-ready, a fair number of security holes, and he kept hitting things that were broken or missing every time he opened it. For a first version, fine. For $40k and three months, a bad trade.

Then I rebuilt the same app with AI in about two weeks, and it came out cleaner than what he'd paid for.

So if you're non-technical and sitting on an idea, I don't think hiring a cheap dev team is the move anymore. Tools like Lovable, v0, and bolt.new get you a working version fast - enough to put in front of real users and see if anyone actually wants it. That's the question to answer before you spend real money on engineering.

One catch: AI only helps if you know how to drive it. A vague prompt gets you the same mess. Used well, it now out-builds a big share of the developers you'd hire for this kind of work.

If you're non-technical, would you trust an AI-built MVP to test your idea, or does that still feel too risky?
