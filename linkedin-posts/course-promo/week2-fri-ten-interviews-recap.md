Ten interviews, one weekend of outreach, zero code. That's usually enough to know whether an idea is worth building.

The hard part isn't finding the ten people. It's what you ask them.

Ask about the past, not the future. "Would you use this?" just buys you a polite yes. The question that works is "walk me through the last time you hit this problem" - people can only answer that with what they actually did. A compliment from a friend isn't a signal; they're just being kind.

Then make the ten conversations decide something. Score each one against the same bar, and let the pattern tell you whether to keep going or kill it - before you spend a cent on code.
